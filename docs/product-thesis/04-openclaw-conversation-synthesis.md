The OpenClaw conversation corpus reinforces the same thesis from an operational
angle.

## What the conversations are saying

- Build a local-first cognitive system that catches raw thought before it
  evaporates.
- Treat fragments, transcripts, and loose notes as inputs, not as the final
  object.
- Preserve context, provenance, and correction paths so the system stays
  inspectable.
- Personalize by reasoning style and working preferences, not by generic
  segmentation.
- Keep the public surface lightweight and useful; do not force the user to
  manage the machinery.
- Separate private cognition from distribution and monetization concerns until
  the product is stable.

## What is worth keeping

- The core idea of a private cognitive layer that turns saved conversation into
  reusable structure.
- The user model of a burst thinker or strategic synthesizer who loses useful
  material without capture.
- The privacy and sovereignty stance: user thoughts should remain under user
  control.
- The interaction principle: low-friction capture, automatic structuring, and
  explicit review.
- The design emphasis on visual navigation when it helps inspection and recall.

## What should stay out of the thesis

- Repeated agency and pricing language that does not change the product
  behavior.
- Movement-style framing that is persuasive but not operationally specific.
- Duplicate rephrasings of the same product idea.
- Any claim that is not tied to a concrete outcome, review path, or user-visible
  behavior.
