# Algorithm Selection

## Why these algorithms

The Substack research showed that successful niche long-form posts usually do four things well:

1. they orient quickly
2. they break the body into digestible moves
3. they front-load proof
4. they end in synthesis rather than drift

The project adds stricter requirements:

- inspectable meaning layer
- bounded ambiguity
- explicit tensions
- evidence before elegance
- reusable reasoning primitives

That combination makes these the best-fit substrate algorithms.

## Selected algorithms

### 1. `signal-frame-extractor`

Use for:

- headline promise
- subtitle contract
- opening compression

Why:

- the article must know what it is really about before it grows

### 2. `object-model-projection`

Use for:

- section planning
- field mapping
- identifying the article's moving parts

Why:

- deep-thinker content often has multiple interacting objects, not one flat topic

### 3. `transformation-path-extractor`

Use for:

- reading flow
- section order
- "what changes as the article progresses"

Why:

- good long-form writing has progression, not just accumulation

### 4. `decision-commitment-extractor`

Use for:

- conclusions
- defaults
- "what this article is actually committing to"

Why:

- strong long-form content should not end in pure atmosphere

### 5. `guardrail-antigoal-extractor`

Use for:

- anti-genericity rules
- preserving boundaries
- keeping the piece from over-claiming

Why:

- this project should not drift into soft thought-leadership sludge

### 6. `tension-map-extractor`

Use for:

- stakes
- contrast
- pressure in the middle of the piece

Why:

- tension is often what keeps long-form writing alive

### 7. `evidence-confidence-scaffold`

Use for:

- example selection
- citation density
- differentiating grounded claims from interpretive leaps

Why:

- readers need support without losing flow

### 8. `open-question-missingness-extractor`

Use for:

- bounded uncertainty
- explicit unknowns
- review pressure

Why:

- deep thinkers often trust a piece more when it shows what remains unsettled

### 9. `reasoning-primitive-detector`

Use for:

- the transfer layer
- reusable pattern naming
- making the article useful outside its immediate topic

Why:

- the project wants substrate, not just summaries

## Conditional algorithm

### `contradiction-pair-extractor`

Use only when:

- the source has a true contradiction
- the article benefits from naming that contradiction directly

Do not use by default when the source only contains:

- tradeoffs
- mood shifts
- incomplete alignment
